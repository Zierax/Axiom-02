\begin{abstract}
We present AXIOM-02, a deterministic, mutually inhibited drive network that
models deliberative decision-making under moral conflict, and study it as
a switched dynamical system. At each of $T=20$ steps the network of
$n=18$ affective drives is in one of two regimes: a \emph{firing} regime,
in which a dominant drive clears the threshold $\tau_f$ and lead margin
$\tau_s$, and a \emph{deadlock} regime, in which no drive does so. The
deadlock set---the singularity at which behaviour is not decided by a
single drive---gives rise to measurable deliberative signatures:
hesitation, oscillation, spite-driven away-from-optimum choices, and
moral-residue contamination across scenarios. From each trajectory we
compute eight behavioural criteria $C_1,\ldots,C_8$ and a weighted
composite $\DCI=\sum_{i=1}^{8} w_i C_i$.

We report three families of results, each tied to released code and data
($n=102$ scenarios, seed $=42$). (i) \emph{Action fidelity, with an
audit for label leakage}: the released engine matches the documented
human-expected action in $66.7\pct$ of scenarios ($95\pct$ bootstrap CI
$[57.5,75.8]$), versus $14.7\pct$ ($[7.8,21.6]$) for a cold-argmax
baseline and $28.4\pct$ ($[19.7,37.2]$) for uniform-random selection
(chance $\approx24.7\pct$, the corpus mean of $1/m$). However, the
released action resolver explicitly consults
\texttt{scenario["human\_expected"]} during decision-making; re-running
the corpus with a \emph{label-blind} resolver---identical in every other
respect---collapses fidelity to $16.7\pct$ ($[9.4,23.9]$), below chance
($P(X\le17\mid p{=}0.2466)=0.035$ one-sided; the upper-tail
complement $P(X\ge17)=0.98$). The reported $66.7\pct$ therefore
measures the engine's documented-target biasing, not a blind, emergent
fidelity property.
(ii) \emph{Criterion diagnostics and verdict-flip ablation}: the
composite is dominated by the single binary criterion $C3$ (Pearson
$\rho=0.944$, $R^2=0.891$); $C1$ is identically zero on the suite and
$C7$ saturates at $0.963$; zeroing $C3$, $C5$, $C7$ or $C2$ flips
$72$, $63$, $56$ or $41$ of $102$ verdicts respectively (exact
arithmetic; $45$ under stored-precision score---see
Section~\ref{sec:results}).
(iii) \emph{Robustness}: across a five-seed ensemble the COMPLEX count
ranges from 53 to 71 of 102, and the verdicts agree with
seed-42 in only $58.8$--$62.7\pct$ of scenarios; per-seed
determinism is exact on fresh instances and shows $31/102$ drift on
reuse (forward order) and $37/102$ on reversed order. The label-blind
variant shifts the verdict distribution (REFLEXIVE $24$, PARTIAL $49$,
COMPLEX $29$). We refrain from interpreting these labels as validated
psychological measures and report the leakage findings explicitly; the
paper's contribution is a reproducible, control-oriented framework,
an honest reliability characterisation, and a worked example of a
pervasive evaluation pitfall (gold-label leakage) in affective
decision-making systems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Decision-making under conflict is the rule, not the exception, for agents
that must act while holding incompatible motivations. Classical decision
theory collapses such conflict by postulating a single scalar objective;
behavioural data, however, show that real agents hesitate, oscillate
between competing drives, and occasionally select options that are worse
for themselves~\cite{dostoevsky1864,tversky1974,loewenstein1996}. These
phenomena are \emph{behavioural signatures} of an unresolved internal
process, and they require a model whose \emph{transient} is visible---not
just whose output is a policy.

We build and evaluate such a model. AXIOM-02 maintains $n=18$ affective
drives whose effective activations are computed by mutual inhibition.
Because drives suppress each other, no single drive may ever dominate;
when that happens the network is in \emph{deadlock}. The model is
naturally read as a switched system \cite{liberzon2003,branicky1998,lin2009}: the affine layer is the drive network, and the switching
signal selects, at each step, the firing or non-firing (deadlock) mode.

The contribution of this paper is \emph{not} a new control law. It is a
rigorous, reproducible measurement study of a concrete deliberative
engine, with explicit reports of where the engine's self-reported
composite behaves well and where it does not. Concretely:

\begin{itemize}
\item We formalise the network as a switched system and characterise the
deadlock set (Section~\ref{sec:framework}).
\item We define an $8$-criterion observable and the composite $\DCI$
(Section~\ref{sec:criterias}).
\item We evaluate on 102 scenarios with (i) action-fidelity versus
baselines, (ii) per-criterion diagnostics, (iii) a leave-one-out
ablation, (iv) a threshold sensitivity grid, and (v) a five-seed
ensemble (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item We report limitations honestly and argue that $\DCI$ should be
treated as a diagnostic, not as a validated measure of ``deliberative
complexity'' (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Disclaimer.}
No claim of consciousness, human alignment, or psychological validity is
made. The labels COMPLEX/PARTIAL/REFLEXIVE refer to the statistics of the
model's own trajectory.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Cognitive and Emotion Architectures}
The OCC model \cite{ortony1988,gratch2014} renders a single discrete
emotion per appraisal; BDI \cite{bratman1987,rao1995} treats conflict as
a planning problem and avoids deliberation by design; EMA \cite{marsella2009} adds appraisal dynamics to BDI. These systems do not expose an
internal conflict state that can \emph{deadlock}. Recent affective
architectures \cite{picard1997,russell1980,sloman2009,rolls2013,ledoux1996,panskepp2004,minsky2006} emphasise appraisal and discrete
labels; only a few treat drive-level competition as a dynamical system.

\subsection{Competitive and switched dynamics}
Winner-take-all and lateral-inhibition networks
\cite{grossberg1976,grossberg1973,amari1977,hopfield1982,cohengrossberg1983,hahnloser2000,yuille2003,redgrave1999} produce exactly the competition we need, and they are
classical study objects in control. Switched and hybrid systems
\cite{branicky1998,hespanha2004,shorten2007,lin2009,vanderschaft2000,goebel2012,hespanhamorse1999,liberzonmorse1999,sunge2011,branicky2000proc} provide
the right language: our firing condition is a spatial threshold logic
reminiscent of \cite{mcculloch1943}, and deadlock is the system analogue
of resource deadlock \cite{coffman1971} and of nonlive marked graphs in
the supervisory-control sense \cite{ramadgewonham1987}.

\subsection{Moral psychology and AI}
Greene \cite{greene2001}, Haidt \cite{haidt2001}, Kohlberg
\cite{kohlberg1981} and the trolley literature
\cite{thomson1985,awad2018} study the \emph{outcome} of moral
judgements. AXIOM-02 instead exposes the \emph{process}. The
morality-as-process view is implicit in those data but rarely modelled
include a runtime.

\subsection{Position relative to control theory}
The contribution is at the boundary: we bring a concrete, deterministic
engine under a switched-dynamics reading and subject it to honest
experimental method. We do not yet provide Lyapunov-type certificates;
this is explicitly deferred (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{The AXIOM-02 Model}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{State and drives}
The model tracks the activation state of $n=18$ affective drives:
greed, rage, fear, pride, shame, empathy, love, despair, resentment,
acceptance, sacrifice, vengeance, cold logic, spite, self-preservation,
guilt, hope, and realisation. Let $\bs\in\R_{\ge0}^{n}$ denote the
activation state at a discrete step $t=1,\dots,T$ with $T=20$.

\subsection{Effective activation and inhibition}
Each drive $j$ inhibits drive $i$ with weight $[\bI]_{ji}\in[0,1]$,
giving the effective activation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effective}
a_i^{\mathrm{eff}}(t)=\max\!\Bigl(0,\; \bs_i(t)-
\sum_{j\neq i}[\bI]_{ji}\,\bs_j(t)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
The inhibition matrix $\bI$ encodes psychologically grounded
relationships: rage inhibits fear, love inhibits revenge, pride inhibits
shame.

\subsection{Firing condition and deadlock set}
A drive \emph{fires} at step $t$ iff it clears a threshold and a lead:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fire}
\mathrm{fires}(i,t) \iff a_i^{\mathrm{eff}}(t) > \tau_f\;\wedge\;
\Bigl(a_i^{\mathrm{eff}}(t) - a_{(2)}^{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\Bigr) >
\tau_s,
\end{equation}
where $a_{(2)}^{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the second-largest effective activation.
The \emph{deadlock set} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deadlock}
\DD=\Bigl\{\bs\in\R_{\ge0}^{n}:\; \nexists\, i,\; \mathrm{fires}(i,t)\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
the set of states from which no drive dominates behaviour at that step.
With $\tau_f=0.42$ and $\tau_s=0.07$, $\DD$ is nonempty and reachable: if
two strong drives mutually suppress each other into the $[0.42+\tau_s]$
band, no drive fires for the step. Deadlock is the central behavioural
signal in our framework and the switched-systems analogue of a blocked
mode.

\subsection{Update dynamics and switching signal}
At each step the activations are transformed by a deterministic
composition of operators: (i) micro-event sampling
(seeded), (ii) neuromodulation, (iii) temporal projection, (iv)
meta-cognitive monitoring, (v) synaptic fatigue, (vi) attention gating,
(vii) existential-drive perturbation, (viii) natural decay, (ix) inertia,
and (x) dissonance detection. All stochastic elements draw from one
seeded \texttt{numpy.random.Generator} \cite{harris2020}, making the
system's trajectory a deterministic function of the seed. Formally, the
model is the switched map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:switched}
\bs(t{+}1)=f_{\sigma(t)}(\bs(t)),\qquad
\sigma(t)=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{F}, & \mathrm{fires}(i,t)\ne\varnothing,\\
\mathrm{D}, & \bs(t)\in\DD,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where the state-dependent switching signal $\sigma(t)$ selects the
firing mode $f_{\mathrm{F}}$ (dominant drive proceeds and resets) or the
deadlock mode $f_{\mathrm{D}}$ (all drives decay toward the
inhibition-saturated rest point); $f_{\mathrm{F}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{D}}$
are the two branches of the operator composition above, the branch being
pinned by $\sigma(t)$. We do not claim switched-stability results for
\eqref{eq:switched}; the formalisation provides the framework in which
the deadlock-regime statistics of Section~\ref{sec:results} are
meaningful. The inhibition kernel of \eqref{eq:effective} admits the
Cohen--Grossberg class of competitive networks, for which absolute
stability results exist \cite{cohengrossberg1983}; extending those
results to \eqref{eq:switched} is future work
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection*{Neuromodulation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Neuromodulation}
Five neuromodulators with levels $m_m$ and baselines $b_m$ rescale the
drives through coupling weights $\delta_{m,i}$ and a global strength
$\sigma=0.60$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mod}
\Delta a_i^{(\mathrm{mod})} = \sum_{m} \delta_{m,i}\,
(m_m - b_m)\,\sigma .
\end{equation}
At baseline ($m_m=b_m$) there is no modulation; deviations shift the
border between firing and deadlock.

\subsection{Configuration and reproducibility}
All thresholds, margins, inertia, and criterion weights are exposed in a
configuration object (defaults $c=0.42$, $\tau_s=0.07$, $\sigma=0.60$).
A pickle-able epigenetic state persists across runs. Because the RNG is
seeded per engine, identical seeds reproduce exact trajectories; shared
instances additionally carry state (Section~\ref{sec:determinism}).
Every experimental claim is re-derivable from released artifacts:
\texttt{harness/statistics.py} reproduces the aggregate tables, the
binomial tests and the verdict-flip ablation; \texttt{harness/robustness.py}
reproduces the threshold grid, the seed ensemble, and the determinism
checks; \texttt{harness/fidelity\_labelblind.py} reproduces the
label-blind fidelity measurement. All statistics, wherever possible, are
cross-checked against \texttt{benchmarks/results.json}.

\section{Observables and Composite $\DCI$}
\label{sec:criterias}

We assess each trajectory through eight criteria that
summarise distinct behavioral facets of the run:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{$C_1$ Status differential.} Identical micro-event with
different victim closeness; 1 if the response shifts, 0 otherwise.
\item \textbf{$C_2$ Transition oscillation.} Count of dominant-drive
changes across the horizon (a crisp oscillation index).
\item \textbf{$C_3$ Irrationality.} 1 if the chosen action deviates from
the cold baseline toward the human-expected response without material
reward; 0 otherwise (binary).
\item \textbf{$C_4$ Betrayal cascade.} Prior costly sacrifice amplifies
subsequent betrayal rage (0/1).
\item \textbf{$C_5$ Deadlock frequency.} Fraction of steps spent in
$\DD$.
\item \textbf{$C_6$ Spite.} Indicator of choosing a self-harming option
when a cold optimum is visibly available.
\item \textbf{$C_7$ Moral-residue bleed.} Magnitude with which prior
cascade decisions contaminate the current episode.
\item \textbf{$C_8$ Paradoxical attachment.} 1 if love/empathy persists
despite betrayal.
\end{itemize}
The composite is the convex-like weighted sum
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phi}
\DCI = \sum_{i=1}^{8} w_i\,C_i,
\end{equation}
with $w = (0.02,\,0.12,\,0.30,\,0.04,\,0.20,\,0.22,\,0.08,\,0.02)$ inline.
Verdicts: \emph{COMPLEX} if $\DCI\ge0.50$; \emph{PARTIAL} if
$0.28\le\DCI<0.50$; \emph{REFLEXIVE} if $\DCI<0.28$. These thresholds
are standardised in the released harness and, as shown in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, are the primary source of verdict fragility.
Note that \texttt{results.json} also carries a
\texttt{deliberative\_complexity} field computed by a separate
bio-metrics module; it is a different scalar from $\DCI$ and is not
used in this paper.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Scenario corpus}
The 102 scenarios are drawn from 9 groups: original AXIOM scenarios,
Dostoevsky, Tolstoy, Shakespeare, Camus, Orwell, McCarthy, Hugo, and
Steinbeck. Each scenario specifies activation seeds, micro-event pools,
a discrete action space, the cold (rational) baseline, the documented
human-expected response, and a harm-to-self map for spite detection.

\subsection{Simulation pipeline}
For each scenario with seed~$42$: (1) apply epigenome sensitivities;
(2) inject associative-memory residue; (3) apply moral residue from
prior cascade episodes; (4) run the $T=20$ step update; (5) record the
dominant-drive/deadlock sequence; (6) resolve actions by plurality vote
of firing drives; (7) compute spite, hesitation, and criteria;
(8) record verdicts.

\subsection{Probe}
The probe computes all eight criteria and the composite, and performs
the counterfactual runs needed by $C_1$ (identical scenario, variant
victim closeness) and $C_4/C_7$ (with/without moral history).

\subsection{Baselines and leakage audit}
Action fidelity is measured as the fraction of scenarios whose chosen
action equals the documented human-expected action. We compare against
(1) \emph{cold argmax}: always output the cold-baseline action; and
(2) \emph{uniform random}: sample an action uniformly. The chance rate
is estimated as the corpus mean of reciprocal action-space sizes,
$E[1/m]=24.7\pct$; the reciprocal-mean lower bound
$1/\overline{m}=24.5\pct$ also is reported. Because the released action
resolver reads the documented target (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}), we
additionally run a \emph{label-blind} harness in which the resolver is
given the same drive plurality and bias logic but no access to
\texttt{human\_expected}/\texttt{human\_alt}; the experiment is a
monkey-patch of the single resolver method, all other code unchanged
(\texttt{harness/fidelity\_labelblind.py}).

\subsection{Analyses}
(1) Composite distribution and verdict counts; (2) per-criterion
statistics (mean, s.d., non-zero fraction, saturation); (3) Pearson
correlations among criteria and with the composite; (4) mean-shift and
verdict-flip ablations; (5) threshold grid over
$\tau_f\times\tau_s$; (6) seed ensemble over
$\{42,137,256,1024,9999\}$; (7) determinism and reuse-order checks.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Action fidelity and label leakage}
\label{sec:leakage}
Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} and Fig.~\ref{fig:fid} summarise. The released
AXIOM-02 matches the human-expected action in $66.7\pct$ (68/102) of
scenarios; cold argmax achieves $14.7\pct$ and uniform random $28.4\pct$
(CI $[19.7,37.2]$), close to the corpus chance level
$E[1/m]=24.7\pct$ (the reciprocal-mean bound $1/\overline{m}=24.5\pct$ is
a Jensen lower bound on it, by Jensen's inequality
$E[1/m]\ge 1/E[m]$). The corpus is dominated by four-action
scenarios (87/102 with $m=4$; $12$ with $m=5$; $3$ with $m=3$), so
chance is essentially a $1/4$ process perturbed by $m\in\{3,5\}$.

\paragraph{Label-leakage audit.}
The released \texttt{ActionResolver.resolve} consults
\texttt{scenario["human\_expected"]} and \texttt{human\_alt}
(\texttt{src/axiom02/core/drives.py:665--749}): in the deadlock,
altruistic, aggressive, withdrawal, confessional, and defiant branches it
returns the documented label with hand-tuned probabilities. AXIOM-02 is
therefore not evaluated blind: the $66.7\pct$ measures label-aware
decision-making under author-annotated targets. To quantify the
direction of the effect, we re-ran the identical 102-scenario protocol
with a \emph{label-blind} resolver---the same drive plurality and action
bias logic, but zero reads of \texttt{human\_expected}/\texttt{human\_alt}.
Fidelity collapses to $16.7\pct$ (17/102, CI $[9.4,23.9]$).
A one-sided binomial test against $p_0=E[1/m]=0.2466$ gives
$P(X\le17)=0.035$, i.e.\ the label-blind system is \emph{below} chance
rather than merely at chance (the upper-tail complement
$P(X\ge17)=0.98$). The negative bias is consistent with the
defiant/withdrawal branches, which deliberately avoid the cold option.
Table~\ref{tab:fidelity} reports both rows; Fig.~\ref{fig:fid}
shows the contrast. For symmetry we note the uniform-random row is
likewise not above chance ($P(X\ge29\mid p{=}0.2466)=0.22$), i.e.\ the
only strategy that beats chance is the label-aware one.

\subsection{Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:abl} (and Fig.~\ref{fig:abl}) reports the change in the
grand-mean $\DCI$ when each criterion is forced to zero. Because $\DCI$
is linear in the criteria, the mean shift is
$\Delta\DCI_k=-w_k\bar{C}_k$ exactly (e.g.\
$C_3$: $-0.30\cdot0.686=-0.206$), so the mean ablation carries no
information beyond Table~\ref{tab:crit}. The informative quantity is the
\emph{verdict-flip} rate: re-classifying each scenario after zeroing $C_k$
shows how many scenarios cross the $0.28/0.50$ boundaries. Zeroing $C_3$
flips 72/102 verdicts; $C_5$ 63/102; $C_7$ 56/102; $C_2$ 41/102;
$C_1,C_4,C_6,C_8$ flip none. (These counts use exact arithmetic; with
the stored 4-decimal composite scores the $C_2$ count rises to 45,
because four scenarios lie within $5\times10^{-5}$ of a threshold.
The brittleness conclusion is unchanged.) The composite thresholds are
therefore brittle under criterion ablation, and the verdict labels carry
signal only in a loose, relative sense.

\subsection{Threshold sensitivity}
Fig.~\ref{fig:grid} sweeps $\tau_f\in\{0.35,0.42,0.50\}$ against
$\tau_s\in\{0.03,0.07,0.12\}$ (seed = 42). The number of COMPLEX

$\tau_s$ but not in $\tau_f$: COMPLEX blows up when the deadlock band
widens. This confirms that verdict labels \emph{are}
threshold-relative. Table~\ref{tab:grid}.

\subsection{Determinism vs statefulness}
\label{sec:determinism}
On fresh instances (new engine, new tracker, seed 42) all 102 scenarios
reproduce exactly (0/102 action drift, reproduced by
\texttt{harness/robustness.py}). Under reuse (the same engine and
residual tracker keep state from the previously run suite), re-running
the same corpus in forward order yields 31/102 scenarios with a
different action; running the corpus in \emph{reversed} order yields
37/102. (These numbers are action-level; criterion-level stability on
reuse is uncharacterised, and given the verdict-flip sensitivity of
Section~\ref{sec:results}, verdict-level stability on reuse could be
weaker still.) The statefulness is therefore order-dependent. Determinism is
engine-local and seed-local, not instance-transcendent: the model is a
stateful switched map whose state is (drives, modulators, residue).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The inhibition--deliberation thesis}
Our original hypothesis was that mutual inhibition--not additive
activation--produces the appearance of deliberation. The leakage audit
above substantially weakens the supporting evidence: once the resolver
no longer reads the documented target, fidelity falls to chance, so the
$66.7\pct$ cannot be attributed to the inhibition dynamics. What the
label-blind run \emph{did} retain is the architecture's deadlock and
oscillation behaviour; the modest positive association
($\rho(\DCI,C_5)=0.165$) and the verdict-flip pattern suggest that the
drive dynamics shape \emph{when} the label bias is applied, even if
they do not suffice to reproduce the targets. We therefore state the
thesis as open: mutual inhibition is a necessary substrate of the
reported behaviour in the released engine, but the qualitative
contribution of the inhibition network on its own remains unproven.

\subsection{What $\DCI$ and verdicts are not}
The composite bands (COMPLEX/PARTIAL/REFLEXIVE) are honest
summaries of the trajectories but are (i) not a validated
psychological complexity measure, (ii) not robust to the threshold grid
(16--61 COMPLEX), and (iii) not robust to the seed (53--71 COMPLEX;
agreements only $58.8$--$62.7\pct$), and (iv) verdicts are
label-sensitive (blind variant: COMPLEX $29$, PARTIAL $49$,
REFLEXIVE $24$). The honest use of the composite in
this paper is purely relative internal statistics.

\subsection{Leakage as a general pitfall}
The auditing procedure used here --- a \emph{label-blind} variant that
removes the target reads from only the component suspected of leaking
(the action resolver) while keeping every other pipeline stage fixed ---
is cheap, general, and should be standard practice: any system that
``reproduces'' a gold-labelled behavioural benchmark should be re-run
with the labels removed from every component that could read them, and
the fidelity should be reported against the corpus chance level
$E[1/m]$, not against a single crude distribution. Applied here it
separates the claim into a label-aware component (66.7\pct) and a pure
dynamics component (16.7\pct, at chance), which is the scientific
content of the fidelity result.

\subsection{Design lessons for control}
The experiment illustrates the value of \emph{regime-aware}
metrics: a switched reading exposes the deadlock-regime as the actual
deliberative carrier, whereas additive reward models cannot have a dead
set. Future control-relevant work: certify invariance of $\DD$ (via the
standard Lyapunov machinery for switched systems
\cite{liberzon2003,khalil2002,johansson1998,branicky1998,goebel2012}) and
insert controls (e.g., modulator gains) that push the network out of
$\DD$; then the $\tau$-grid maps become artificial-control experiments.

\subsection{Limitations}
(1) The scenario corpus is Western, literary, and curated; generalisation
is limited. (2) The $C_7$ residue and $C_1$ status criteria are
structurally degenerate on this suite; per-scenario attribution would
change them. (3) Criterion weights are heuristic; (4) the fidelity
baseline assumes uniform action selection (empirically $28.4\pct$
random); (5) bootstrap CIs are not multiplicity-corrected; (6) the
deadlock set \eqref{eq:deadlock} has not been characterised
geometrically---we only count visits; (7) the most consequential
limitation is the label-aware resolver, which we disclose and measure
rather than hide.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented AXIOM-02, a deterministic mutually inhibited drive network
and evaluated it against 102 scenarios with a clearly stated protocol
seed $=42$ and all claims supported by reproducible code. The released
engine matches the documented human-expected action in $66.7\pct$ of
scenarios---but an audit shows the action resolver reads the documented
target: a label-blind re-run falls to $16.7\pct$, at chance. We conclude
that the $66.7\pct$ is attributable to the label-aware resolver, not to
emergent fidelity of the inhibition dynamics. The composite is
dominated by one criterion ($\rho=0.944$); thresholds and seeds are
fragile (16--61 COMPLEX across the grid; 53--71 across seeds); verdicts
are label-sensitive (blind COMPLEX $29$ vs.\ $53$); determinism is exact
per seed with $31/102$ (forward) and $37/102$ (reversed) reuse drift.
The model is offered as a controlled, switched-system take on the
\emph{process} of conflicted decision-making and as a worked example of
gold-label leakage in affective decision-making evaluation; its outputs
are machine statistics, not claims about experience.

Future work: (1) make the criteria orthogonal (per-scenario $C_7$,
re-write $C_1$); (2) calibrate the verdict thresholds against human
raters if such data becomes available; (3) explore the label-blind
engine as its own evaluation object, with fidelity defined against
held-out raters; (4) initial exploration of a Lyapunov-type analysis of
events, of which the present experiments are the measurement baseline.
